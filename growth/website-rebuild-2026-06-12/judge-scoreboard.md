# Judge scoreboard — 8 landing variants

Five judges, each scoring /10. Max 50.

**Judges:** Skeptical CFO (ROI, proof, distrust of fluff) · Midnight Founder (5-second clarity while half-asleep and scrolling) · Competitor (what's hard to copy) · Ideal Customer (micro-SMB owner-operator — "is this for me, do I trust it, what does it cost") · Conversion Copywriter (hook, flow, friction, CTA).

## Judge commentary

**V1 — Tool Gap (41).**
- CFO: "Acknowledges money already being spent — that's a real budget line I can reallocate, not new spend. 8."
- Founder: "It's 11pm, I pay for three AI tools, and none of them did my invoices today. This headline reads my mind. 9."
- Competitor: "The insight is sharp but I could copy the line tomorrow. 7."
- Customer: "True for me — I literally pay £200/month for Claude and still do admin by hand. 8."
- Copywriter: "Best hook of the eight: specific, mildly accusatory, instantly self-qualifying. The reader who doesn't pay for AI bounces — that's fine, they're not the customer. 9."

**V6 — Spreadsheet (41).**
- CFO: "Concrete inputs and outputs. I can audit this promise. 8."
- Founder: "'Your whole business lives in a spreadsheet' — guilty. Slightly less punchy than V1 but I see myself immediately. 8."
- Competitor: "The ledger diagram + named flows is actual product thinking, not copy. Hardest to imitate credibly. 8."
- Customer: "Highest score I'll give. This describes my Tuesday. The named flow 'job sheet → invoices → Xero' is literally my life. 9."
- Copywriter: "'Bring your spreadsheet to the call' is the best CTA of the set — it lowers friction by giving the prospect a job they can already do. 8."

**V8 — Anti-Agency (39).**
- CFO: "'Cancel if it doesn't pay for itself' is a real risk-reversal. Wanted numbers earlier. 7."
- Founder: "Negative hooks need me to know the cliché being mocked. At midnight I might not. 7."
- Competitor: "The guardrails section (approve button, accountant untouched, drafts never auto-send) is the most defensible thing in all eight pages — it's operational truth, not copywriting. 9."
- Customer: "The guardrails answer my actual fear: that a robot sends a wrong invoice to my best client. 8."
- Copywriter: "Strong differentiation, strong close. Weak open. 8."

**V2 — Assistant (38).**
- CFO: "The cost ledger vs a £900/mo part-time admin is the single best ROI argument anywhere in this exercise. 9."
- Founder: "Good, but I have to do comparison maths in my head at the hero stage. 7."
- Competitor: "Every AI agency runs a 'cheaper than hiring' line. 6."
- Customer: "Price clarity up front earns trust. 8."
- Copywriter: "Anchor is excellent mid-page; weak as a headline. 8."

**Kills.**
- **V3 (27):** Generic — could be any agency in any decade. Competitor judge: "I *am* this page." Killed.
- **V4 (31):** Best emotional beat ("It's 11pm and you're doing invoices again") but no spine of proof; CFO killed it. One line survives into the final.
- **V5 (31):** Trend-rider. The ideal customer doesn't self-identify as a "one-person + AI business" — he identifies as a chauffeur company. Killed; the setup-gap insight survives inside V1's section.
- **V7 (29):** Makes the reader do homework before earning attention. Killed; the £6k/year admin-cost stat survives as a supporting line.

## Merge decision — what the final page takes

1. **Hook + "the gap" section from V1** (highest hook scores; self-qualifying).
2. **Concrete pipeline + named flows + diagram from V6** (customer's favourite; hardest to copy).
3. **Guardrails + risk-reversal from V8** (answers the trust objection; most defensible).
4. **Cost-ledger pricing from V2** (CFO's best ROI case, placed at the pricing section, not the hero).
5. Surviving lines: V4's "It's 11pm…" as section copy; V7's £6k/year stat as support; V5's setup-gap framing folded into the gap section.

**Why this wins:** V1 stops the right reader, V6 makes the promise concrete enough to believe, V8 removes the fear of saying yes, V2 makes the price feel obvious. Each killed page died for the same root cause — it asked the reader to either do work (V7), recognise a trend (V5), or accept a generic promise (V3, V4) before giving them a reason to trust.
